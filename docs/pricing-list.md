## 5. Recommended presentation order

When quoting a full price list to a prospect, present it in this order (workshopped Nov 2025 to remove confusion):

1. **45-minute PT session only (no online programming)** — anchors the standalone rate first
2. **Online Programming ($22/wk) + 45-minute PT session** — shows what changes when bundled
3. **Online Programming only** — for anyone who just wants structure and accountability

Leading with PT-only pre-empts the "why is the bundle cheaper?" question. Don't split app access and online programming into separate lines — they are the same thing, and listing them separately reads as two charges.

## 6. Positioning and rationale

- **Frequency discounts run the opposite way to most PTs.** Karl's position: don't reduce the per-session rate for higher weekly frequency. Clients who train 3x/week were going to do so largely regardless of price, and discounting devalues the service. Set the base rate at the minimum you'd accept, then add a premium for lower-frequency/less-committed options. That produces a tiered structure without ever undervaluing your time.
- **Legacy rates are hard to undo.** Once a client is on a discounted rate for an open-ended period, it is very difficult to bring them back up. Prefer moving clients to current rates at a natural transition point (change of arrangement, new year, change of frequency).
- **Free app access** is extended only to family and a couple of close friends. It is a deliberate privilege, not a default.
- **Market context (Brisbane, 2025 assessment):** non-gym-affiliated PTs average $80–$100 for 45–60 min; gym-affiliated PTs $65–$90 without app-based programming; online coaching ranges $15–$80/wk. Karl's differentiators are tertiary qualifications in exercise science *and* behavioural science, plus habit/behaviour-change coaching rather than sessions alone.
