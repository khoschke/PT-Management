# PT development: coaching observations and how Karl wants to run it

Karl's working notes for the fortnightly PT development session. Not
trainer-facing, and deliberately separate from the portal's development notes,
which are a shared coaching record both sides can read.

That distinction matters. An observation you hold about someone and a
conversation you have with them are different things. What is written here is
the first. Only the parts Karl chooses to raise become the second.

## 21 September 2026: the conversion rate conversation

Raised with Danny after the stand-up, about Michael and Dylan.

### What was noticed

Both are heavily focused on conversion rate. Both come from bigger-gym
backgrounds, Snap Fitness and Goodlife respectively.

### The mismatch behind it

In those environments a sales manager handed a trainer a client who had
already bought a session pack. The trainer's job started at the first session,
and conversion rate was the right number to watch, because the acquisition had
already happened somewhere else.

Fitaz Gym does not work that way and is not going to. Here, a self-employed
trainer generates their own clients. The equivalent of that sales manager's job
is theirs now: calling new members and asking whether they want personal
training. That is not an extra burden bolted onto the role, it IS the role, and
it is already built into the process as the day 11 onward nurture flow.

**Karl's own note on this:** he did not see the mismatch at first. The
fixation on conversion rate looked like a preference rather than a symptom of
where they had come from. Worth remembering as a general point, because the
next trainer who arrives from a big-box gym will carry the same assumption and
it will look like something else again.

### The second observation, and its honest status

Michael and Dylan are also the two trainers who spend the least time on the gym
floor. If they are not training a client or training themselves, they are not
in the building.

Presence and opportunity are linked. You cannot know whether the next client
was going to appear tomorrow or in a year, and you only find out by being there.
Some of this is a long game and that is fine, but the long game still requires
turning up for it.

**This is an impression held by Karl and Danny, not a measured fact.** Nobody
has counted. If it ever needs to be more than an impression, GymMaster check-in
data would answer it. Worth being careful with, because "you're not here
enough" lands very differently depending on whether it is a feeling or a
figure, and it is currently a feeling.

## The change Karl wants to make in how he manages

The pattern to move away from: a trainer brings a problem, Karl goes away and
solves it, Karl returns with the answer.

The pattern to move towards: Karl makes sure they understand how the system
already works, then asks them how they would use it to solve the problem
themselves.

This is the standard move from consulting to coaching, and the reason it is
worth the effort is that these are self-employed people running their own
businesses. Every problem Karl solves for them is a problem they have not
learned to solve, and they will meet it again without him.

**The caveat Karl has already noticed, and it is a real one:** several of the
systems that now exist came directly out of a trainer raising a problem. The
answer is not to stop solving things. It is to tell the difference between a
problem that reveals a gap in the system, which is Karl's to fix, and a problem
the system already answers, which is theirs to work through with support.

### Questions that do this work in the fortnightly session

- What have you already tried?
- What does the system give you here, and what have you used so far?
- If I was not available this week, what would you do next?
- What would you need to be true to make that work?
- What is the smallest version of that you could try before we next meet?

The one that surfaces a genuine system gap: **"What did you need that wasn't
there?"** That question separates the two cases better than anything else,
because a trainer who can answer it concretely has found a real gap, and one
who cannot is usually describing a problem the system already covers.

## What to do with this

Not a script, and not something to deliver in one sitting. The conversion rate
mismatch is worth raising with Michael and Dylan individually rather than in
the group session, because it is about where each of them came from.

The presence observation should wait until Karl has either decided it does not
need data, or gone and got some.
