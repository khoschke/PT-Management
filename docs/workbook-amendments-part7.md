### 6. Text approach: new cold-lead system and an opt-out line (logged 15 Nov 2026)

**What, part 2:** Under the Spam Act 2003 (enforced by the ACMA, not the Australian Consumer Law), a text that promotes a service is a commercial electronic message, even if the offer is free and even if it's a single text to one person. Every commercial message needs:
- consent (express, or inferred from an existing relationship, such as a gym membership). Covered: the gym already markets to members by email, so marketing consent is captured at sign-up.
- clear identification of the sender (name and Fitaz Gym),
- a functional unsubscribe option. "Reply STOP to opt out" is the standard for SMS. Opt-outs must be honoured within 5 working days, and the option must keep working for at least 30 days after sending.

**Decision (Karl, 15 Nov 2026):** Include "Reply STOP to opt out" in every first-contact text only, not in every reply in an ongoing conversation with a prospect. No lawyer review needed for this one.

**Fix:**
1. Add "Reply STOP to opt out" to the end of the cold, warm and referral first-contact text examples.
2. Add a short note to the section explaining why: these texts promote a service, so the Spam Act applies. Include the line in every first-contact text, and honour and record any opt-out in the PT portal.

### 10. The 2-2-2 rule: portal tracking, permission and opt-outs (logged 15 Nov 2026, updated same day)

**Where:** Part 7 Making Sales, "The 2-2-2 rule for leads who don't convert": the intro, the three touches, the example messages, and the activity "Where will you track your 2-2-2 follow-ups so none slip through?". PT portal and masters.

**What:** The section predates the portal and the Spam Act changes in item 6:
- It tells PTs to note each touch "with the date of your next touch", as if they're tracking it themselves. Follow-ups now live in the PT portal, with automated reminders once item 11 is built.
- It treats a polite no the same as going quiet, and says the sequence "works the same whether they ghosted you or gave you a polite no". That no longer applies.
- The example texts have no opt-out, and the section doesn't say what happens if someone opts out.
- The activity question no longer fits, for the same reason as item 7.

**Decisions (Karl, 15 Nov 2026):**
- **A no is treated the same as a STOP.** No follow-ups after a no unless the lead has agreed to them.
- **Ask permission to clarify the no.** When a lead says no, the PT replies once, in the same conversation: "Totally fine. Mind if I check in down the track, or would you rather I leave it there?"
  - If they say yes to a check-in, the 2-2-2 sequence starts, and every touch is one they agreed to.
  - If they say "leave it", or don't answer the permission question, treat it as a STOP: record it, end the sequence, no further contact about PT.
  - Ask once. The permission question is part of the "no" conversation, never a separate follow-up later.
- **48-hour limit on responding to a no (all lead types, settled 17 Nov 2026; almost always unverified in practice).** The PT has 48 hours from receiving a no to send the permission question. After that, asking would count as pestering, so the window closes and the lead is treated as a STOP. This applies to nos only, not to any other step in the process. 48 hours is the outer limit, not the target: if the no comes on the phone or face to face, ask then and there.
- **Missed windows go to the PT Manager.** If the 48-hour window closes, the PT Manager is notified automatically and follows it up with the PT. The PT Manager can reopen the lead if the close was an accident or the PT simply didn't update the portal in time.
- **Leads who go quiet** (no reply at all to first contact) still get the 2-2-2 sequence, since they haven't said no.
- **Name the end point.** After the 2-month touch with no reply, the lead closes. Say so, so PTs know when to stop.
- **Use a real event in the invite example.** Swap the "free community session Saturday morning" for something Fitaz Gym actually runs, such as the First Friday workout, so PTs can use the example as is.

**Fix:**
1. Rewrite the intro to split the two cases: went quiet (2-2-2 runs) and said no (ask permission once, within 48 hours; yes starts 2-2-2, anything else is a STOP).
2. Replace the 2-day "if they said not right now" line with the permission question.
3. Explain the 48-hour limit on responding to a no: the portal closes the lead automatically if it's missed, and the PT Manager is notified and will follow up. If the PT actually did respond in time but didn't update the portal, they should tell the PT Manager, who can reopen it.
4. Explain the "End communication" button: use it when the prospect has ended the conversation (e.g. "leave it there") to record that the PT has acknowledged it.
5. Replace the self-tracking advice with how follow-ups work in the portal: set the lead's status, and the portal reminds you when each touch is due.
6. Opt-outs: a STOP, a "leave it", no answer to the permission question, or a missed 48-hour window on a no ends everything straight away, is recorded in the portal, and the lead is never contacted again about PT (unless the PT Manager reopens a missed-window close). Say this plainly.
7. STOP line: follow item 6's rule (first-contact texts only). The 2-month reconnect usually lands after weeks of silence, so treat it as a fresh first contact and include "Reply STOP to opt out" on it. The 2-day and 2-week touches continue an existing conversation and don't need it.
8. Update the invite example to a real Fitaz Gym event.
9. Add the end point: after the 2-month touch with no reply, the lead closes.
10. Replace the activity. A possible replacement: "Pick one lead in your portal who went quiet. Which 2-2-2 touch are they due for, and what will you send?"

### 11. PT portal build: automated 2-2-2 follow-up reminders (logged 15 Nov 2026, updated same day)

**Where:** The PT portal app itself (pt-management), not the workbook text.

**What:** PTs currently have to remember their own 2-2-2 follow-ups. Karl wants the portal to remind them automatically, in line with the 2-2-2 rule, the permission decision and the 48-hour limit on nos in item 10.

**Fix:**
1. The sequence starts when either:
   - the lead hasn't replied to first contact (went quiet), or
   - the lead said no, then said yes to a later check-in (status such as "Agreed to check-in").
   It schedules three reminders for the allocated PT: 2 days, 2 weeks and 2 months.
2. Show due follow-ups in the PT's portal view, and add a "Follow-ups due today" line to the daily leads digest email.
3. Add a status for a no that's been clarified as final (e.g. "Declined", treated as an opt-out), so no sequence is ever started for them.
4. **48-hour clock on nos (nos only):** when a PT marks a lead as "Said no", the portal starts a 48-hour clock. Show it in the PT's view and the daily digest (e.g. "Respond to a no: 20 hours left"). If the PT hasn't logged the permission question within 48 hours, the portal closes the lead automatically with a status such as "Ended: no response to a no within 48 hours", treated exactly like a STOP.
5. **Manual "End communication" button (PT):** for when the prospect has ended the conversation and the PT is recording that they've acknowledged it (e.g. the lead said "leave it there"). It records an ended status with the date. Once the 48-hour timer has already closed a lead, the button is unavailable for that lead: the timer's close stands.
6. **PT Manager notification:** when the 48-hour timer closes a lead, the portal automatically emails the PT Manager with the lead's name, the allocated PT, when the no was logged and when the window closed, so the PT Manager can follow it up with the PT. Also list these closes in the PT Manager's daily digest.
7. **PT Manager override:** the PT Manager can reopen a lead that the 48-hour timer closed, for when it was an accident or the PT responded in time but didn't update the portal. Reopening requires a short reason, and the portal logs who reopened it and when. The override applies to timer closes only. It can't reopen a lead that opted out themselves (STOP, "leave it", or a declined permission question).
8. The sequence stops automatically when:
   - the lead replies STOP, says no without agreeing to a check-in, doesn't answer the permission question, or the 48-hour window on a no is missed (opt-out or ended status, recorded with the date, never re-entered into a sequence unless the PT Manager reopens a timer close),
   - the lead books a complimentary session or converts,
   - the 2-month touch is done with no response (lead closes).
9. Once built, update item 10's rewrite to describe the reminders, the 48-hour clock, the PT Manager notification and override as they actually work.

### 13. "Creating certainty: emotional and logical" needs a rewrite (logged 15 Nov 2026)

**Where:** Part 7 Making Sales, "Creating certainty: emotional and logical": the intro, the comparison table and the activity. PT portal and masters.

**What:** Karl flagged the section as not quite right, starting with "why" questions, which can come across as judgemental. A full check found five issues:
1. **"Why" and blame-shaped questions.** "Why does this goal matter to you now?" asks people to justify themselves. "What's stopped you before?" puts the person at the centre of past failures.
2. **"Name what's at stake."** Drifts into fear and pressure, which is avoidance framing and clashes with the workbook's no-pressure approach to consults.
3. **A missing certainty.** The intro says people need certainty about you, the plan and their own ability to stick with it, but the table only covers the first two. Belief in their own ability is the one that matters most for clients who've tried everything before.
4. **"Show results" has no guardrail.** Client stories are useful (with permission), but the section should make clear they show what's possible, not a promised outcome.
5. **"People decide on feeling and justify with reason."** Reads like a technique for working on people. Soften it.

**Fix:**
1. Swap "why" questions for "what" questions, and take blame out of the wording. Suggested:
   - "What's made now the time?"
   - "What's got in the way of it sticking before?"
   - "If this goes well, what's different in six months?"
2. Replace "name what's at stake" with "reflect back what matters to them".
3. Add a third column (or row) for self-belief: the belief is "I can actually do this", built through small early wins and a plan sized to their real week. Possible questions: "What's one thing you've stuck with before, in any part of life?" and "What would make this feel doable in a normal week?"
4. Add a line to the logical column: share client stories, with permission, as examples of what's possible, never as a promise of results.
5. Reword the feeling-and-reason line, e.g. "People need it to feel right and make sense. Hit only the logic and..." (keep the rest of that paragraph).
6. Update the activity to include the third certainty, e.g. "Think of a goal a client of yours might have. What's one emotional, one logical and one self-belief certainty you'd want to build for them?"
7. While rewriting, check the rest of Part 7 for other "why" questions and blame-shaped wording.
